How to make a stop-motion movie on iPhone or iPad

What is stop motion?

Stop motion is animation made from still pictures. You take a picture, move something a tiny bit, and take another. Play the pictures back quickly and your eyes blend them into movement, so a toy that never moved on its own walks across the table. It's the same trick behind clay films like Wallace & Gromit and Coraline, and you can do it with a phone and whatever is in the toy box.

How smooth a movie looks depends on how many pictures play each second. Here's how many pictures a 5-, 10- or 30-second movie takes at each of the app's three speeds:

SpeedPer second5 seconds10 seconds30 seconds
🐢 Slow63060180
🐇 Medium1260120360
🚀 Fast24120240720

Start with Medium. Twelve new pictures a second is what a lot of stop-motion films really use, too: they play at 24 frames a second and show each picture twice, which animators call shooting on twos.

What you need

  • An iPhone or iPad with iOS 26 or iPadOS 26 or later, and Stop Motion Movie Maker, free on the App Store.
  • Something to animate. Action figures, stuffed animals, building-brick minifigures, modeling clay or play dough, puppets, paper cutouts, even socks or fruit. Things that hold a pose are easiest: jointed figures, bricks and clay.
  • A stage. A tabletop against a wall, a shoebox on its side, or a sheet of poster board curved up behind the set so the floor flows into the sky with no line.
  • A lamp. A desk lamp is perfect. Steady light matters more than bright light.
  • Something to hold the camera still. A tripod with a phone mount, a phone or tablet stand, or a stack of books.
  • Nice to have: poster putty to keep characters standing and stick props down, and tape to mark where the phone and the set go.

How to make a stop-motion movie, step by step

Here's the whole process, from an empty table to a movie in Photos. The pictures are from The Mighty Dino, a clay dinosaur and a teddy bear on a felt-and-paper set.

1Plan a tiny story

Keep the first movie short: 5 to 10 seconds, one character, one thing that happens. That's 60 to 120 pictures at Medium speed, enough to finish in one sitting.

A beginning, a middle and an end is plenty. A dinosaur stomps in. It meets a teddy bear. It roars, and the bear falls over. Say it out loud, or draw it in three boxes (that's a storyboard), so you know where each move is going.

2Build the set and light it

Set up on a table that doesn't wobble, against a wall or inside a box. Felt or paper makes good grass, cotton balls make clouds, and a paper sun on the backdrop finishes the scene. Stick props down with putty so they stay put when someone bumps the table.

Light it with a lamp, not the window. Daylight changes every time a cloud passes, and in a movie that shows up as flicker. Close the curtains if you can, and stand where your shadow doesn't fall across the stage.

3Hold the camera still

The camera must not move between pictures, or the whole set will jump around in the movie. Put the iPhone or iPad on a tripod or a stand, or lean it against a stack of books, pointed at the stage.

Hold it upright for a tall movie, the shape of a phone screen, or sideways for a wide one, the shape of a TV. Stop Motion Movie Maker works both ways.

Mark the phone's spot and the set's corners with tape. If something gets bumped, you can put it back exactly where it was.

4Start a new movie and pick a speed

Open Stop Motion Movie Maker and tap New Movie. The camera screen has five big buttons, one job each: Snap, Oops, Play, Ghost and Done.

Tap the speed button at the top of the screen (it shows an animal and a number) and pick 🐢 Slow, 🐇 Medium or 🚀 Fast: 6, 12 or 24 pictures a second.

When you take the first picture, the app locks the camera's focus, exposure and white balance (look for the 🔒), so every picture matches and the movie doesn't flicker.

5Move a little, snap, repeat

Press SNAP! Move your character a tiny bit. Press SNAP! again. That's the whole trick: move, snap, move, snap.

How far is a tiny bit? For a walk, about the width of a fingernail each picture. Smaller moves look slower and smoother; bigger moves look faster.

Press Ghost to see your last picture faintly over the camera. Animators call this onion skinning: you can see exactly where everything was, so each move lines up. Took a picture you don't want? Oops takes it back. Press Play any time to watch the movie so far.

6Keep your hands out of the shot

The most common stop-motion mistake is a hand in the picture, and the second is a bumped camera. Auto Snap fixes both. Turn it on from the + menu, move something, and take your hands away: Auto Snap takes the picture once everything holds still.

It waits while a hand is still in the picture, and it never takes the same picture twice. Nobody touches the phone, so it never wobbles. Prefer to press a button? A Bluetooth camera remote, the volume buttons or a press on an AirPods stem all take a picture too.

7Add words, sounds and music

Tap the pink magic wand to decorate. Add words and speech bubbles that pop in and wiggle, any of 54 sound effects (boings, splats, robots, lasers, a drum roll), and an opening title with a live preview.

With Stop Motion Pro you can also add songs, your own music, a voice-over you record yourself, emoji stickers, video clips, and one-tap looks like Old Movie, Comic and Sketch.

When a character “says” something, take a few pictures without moving anything. The pause gives everyone time to read the bubble.

8Roll the credits and save

Every movie deserves an ending. Choose “The End”, or rolling credits with “Directed by”, “Starring” and anyone else who helped. The credits pick up the song by themselves.

Then tap Save. (Skipping the decorating? Press Done twice on the camera screen.) The movie goes to the Photos app, ready to share, and a fresh movie starts.

Movies also save themselves as you go, so you can stop any time and keep snapping tomorrow. Free movies save at 720p with a small “Made with Stop Motion Movie Maker” badge; Pro saves in 1080p HD or 4K, without it.

Tips for smoother stop motion

  • 🐜 Move less than you think

    Small, even steps look smooth. If the movie looks too fast, the moves are too big.

  • 🚗 Ease in and out

    Start a move with small steps, make the middle steps bigger, and finish small again. Things that speed up and slow down look alive.

  • ⏸️ Hold the first and last pose

    Take about six pictures without moving anything at the start and the end, so the movie doesn't start or stop with a jolt.

  • 💡 Keep the light steady

    Lamp light, curtains closed, and nobody's shadow on the set. Changing light is what makes movies flicker.

  • 🦘 Make things fly

    For a jump, lift the character a little higher each picture on a blob of putty and a pencil hidden behind it, then bring it down again.

  • ▶️ Play it often

    Press Play every few pictures. It's the fastest way to spot a move that's too big, and to see it come alive.

Stop motion in a classroom, library or museum

Stop motion is a great group project: one kid moves the characters, one presses SNAP, one is in charge of sound effects. Stop Motion Movie Maker was inspired by the puppet stations at children's museums, so it has a few things just for groups:

  • Put the stage on a TV. Connect a TV or monitor with a USB-C to HDMI adapter, or use AirPlay. The TV shows the live picture, the ghost and playback, and the iPhone or iPad becomes the remote.
  • Big buttons. Arcade-style button boxes, foot pedals, keyboards and game controllers, including the Xbox Adaptive Controller, can all take pictures.
  • Museum mode. It opens straight into a fresh movie for the next person, and works with Guided Access to keep the iPad in the app.

Stop-motion questions

How many pictures do you need for a stop-motion movie?

Multiply the length in seconds by the pictures per second. At 12 pictures a second, a 10-second movie takes 120 pictures; at 6 a second it takes 60, and at 24 a second it takes 240.

What is the best frame rate for stop motion?

12 pictures a second is the best place to start: smooth enough to look alive and quick enough to finish. Many stop-motion films use 12 new pictures a second too, shown twice each in a 24-frame film, which animators call shooting on twos. Try 6 a second for a jerkier, handmade look, or 24 for very smooth motion.

Why does my stop-motion movie flicker?

Flicker comes from the brightness changing between pictures: daylight from a window, a shadow falling on the set, or the camera adjusting its exposure. Light the set with a lamp, keep away from windows and keep your shadow off the stage. Stop Motion Movie Maker locks focus, exposure and white balance after the first picture so the camera doesn't change between pictures. More on flicker, in Support.

What is onion skinning in stop motion?

Onion skinning shows the last picture faintly over the live camera, so you can see where everything was and move it a small, even step. In Stop Motion Movie Maker it is the Ghost button.

How long does it take to make a stop-motion movie?

A 10-second movie at 12 pictures a second is 120 pictures. At one picture every 15 to 20 seconds, that is 30 to 40 minutes of animating, plus time to set up and decorate. Movies save as you go, so you can stop and finish another day.

Can you make a stop-motion movie with LEGO bricks?

Yes. Building-brick minifigures are great for stop motion because they hold a pose and click onto a baseplate, so they don't slide between pictures. Tape the baseplate to the table so the whole set stays put.

Can kids make a stop-motion movie on their own?

Yes. Stop Motion Movie Maker has five big buttons with one job each and is simple enough for an 8-year-old; younger kids do well with a grown-up setting up the camera. Auto Snap takes pictures hands-free, and nothing can be bought without the Apple Account's confirmation.

Is Stop Motion Movie Maker free?

Yes. It is free to download, with no ads and no account, and you can make as many movies as you like. Free movies save at 720p with a small “Made with Stop Motion Movie Maker” badge. Stop Motion Pro is an optional one-time purchase, not a subscription, for 1080p and 4K, no badge, music, voice-overs, stickers, looks and more.

Can I make stop motion on an iPad?

Yes. Stop Motion Movie Maker works on iPad with iPadOS 26 or later as well as iPhone. The bigger screen is easy for kids to see, and an iPad stand keeps it steady.
